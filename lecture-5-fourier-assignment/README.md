  **Prediction:**

  Because 13.88 Hz is not an integer, it will not align with the 1.0 Hz bin spacing of a 1-second FFT.

I predict the energy will "smear" across adjacent frequency bins (like 13 Hz and 14 Hz), resulting in a wider peak and a recorded amplitude lower than the true 0.4.


**Comparing 1s and 4s spectra:** 
The 1-second recording has a bin spacing of 1 Hz, causing severe spectral leakage for the 13.88 Hz signal. 
The 4-second recording has N = 800, improving the bin spacing to 200 / 800 = 0.25 Hz.

Because 13.88 Hz lands much closer to a 0.25 Hz bin boundary (13.75 or 14.0), the leakage is drastically reduced, and the peak appears narrower and closer to its true amplitude.
 
**Zero-padding behavior:**
       
The zero-padded FFT visually smooths the spectrum curve, making it look similar to the 4-second plot.

However, zero-padding only *interpolates* the existing 1-second frequency data by adding intermediate calculation points.

It does **not** add any new physical information or improve the actual frequency resolution of the underlying signal.



## Exercise 4: Compare the DTFT and DFT


**Comparing DTFT and DFT:** The DFT outputs are discrete samples of the continuous DTFT spectrum. 

The stem markers for both the 4-point and 64-point DFTs fall perfectly on the continuous black curve generated by the DTFT.
**Why fft(x, 64) produces more frequency points:** 

Using N=64 on a 4-sample sequence implicitly pads the time-domain signal with 60 zeros. 

In the frequency domain, this causes the algorithm to evaluate the DTFT curve at 64 densely packed intervals rather than just 4. 

It does **not** add new samples or information to the original physical sequence; it simply increases the visual sampling density of the frequency curve.



  **Where the DTFT magnitude becomes zero:**

Where the DTFT magnitude becomes zero: Looking at the plot, the magnitude drops to exactly zero at w = +- pi/ 2 and at  w = +-pi

This happens because at these specific frequencies, the four samples of the sequence rotate in ways that perfectly cancel each other out when summed together.
